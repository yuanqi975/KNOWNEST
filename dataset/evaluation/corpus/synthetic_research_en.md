# Synthetic evaluation fixture: Meridian Archive migration

> Fictional long-form document written only for KNOWNEST evaluation. It contains no real customer or employee information.

## Background

The Meridian Archive team migrated a small research collection from a legacy catalog to a new catalog over four weekly waves. The migration was deliberately staged so that reviewers could compare records before the final cutover. This narrative is intentionally longer than a typical FAQ and includes repeated operational context that should not be mistaken for the final decision.

During preparation, the team copied records into a read-only staging area. Reviewers were asked to compare titles, authors, and retention labels. A copied record was not considered approved merely because it appeared in the staging area. Approval required a signed review entry in the migration ledger.

## Weekly process

Each Monday, the coordinator published the list of records planned for that week's review. On Tuesday and Wednesday, archivists checked metadata and attached a note when a field could not be verified. On Thursday, a second reviewer sampled the changed records. Friday was reserved for reconciliation and reporting; it was not an automatic publication day.

The migration team tracked three measures: records copied, records reviewed, and records approved. These counts are not interchangeable. A copied record could still be awaiting review, and a reviewed record could still be blocked by a missing retention label. The dashboard displayed all three measures separately to avoid overstating completion.

When a record failed review, the archivist returned it to the correction queue. The source record remained available in the legacy catalog until the final cutover. No record was deleted solely because a copy had been created. The coordinator documented exceptions in the ledger and assigned an owner for follow-up.

## Search and validation notes

Reviewers used title and author fields for the initial lookup, then checked the full record before approval. A short title match was not sufficient evidence when two records had similar names. If a date was missing from a copied record, the reviewer marked it unknown rather than inferring it from a neighboring record.

For cross-wave summaries, the team compared approved records only. Drafts and records in the correction queue were excluded from the final count. If a report did not say whether it included drafts, readers were instructed to treat its total as provisional.

## Final decision

The migration board approved the final cutover on 2026-04-17 after all four weekly waves passed reconciliation. The approved collection contained 8,420 records. The team retained the legacy catalog in read-only mode for 90 days after cutover. A failed reconciliation would have postponed publication; it would not have been waived by the coordinator.

The definitive cutover decision, its date, the approved record count, and the read-only retention period are stated in this final section. Earlier planning notes in this document describe process but do not override the board's final decision.
